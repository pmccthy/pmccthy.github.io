### **Background**

I'm a PhD (DPhil) student at the University of Oxford with a wide range of research interests spanning neuroscience, machine learning and neurotechnology. In my PhD work, I primarily focus on characterising the neural representations underlying flexible, intelligent behaviour in both brains and artificial neural networks. To this end, I analyse high-dimensional neural recordings from the frontal cortex of mice performing value-guided decision making tasks and train RNNs to peform analogous tasks to try and reverse engineer the underlying computations. I am specifically interested in tasks where the value of objects can vary across contexts (such as reversal learning), therefore requiring flexible behaviour. I also develop tools for neuroscience experiments, for example by modelling optogenetic stimulation of V1 neurons and using these models to optimise stimulation parameters. For more details on my PhD work, see my "Projects" section.

I developed my interests in neuroscience, machine learning and neurotechnology whilst studying Biomedical Engineering at Imperial. During this degree, I did computational neuroscience research spanning a range of scales, from developing biophysical models of thalamic circuits to investigate the function of the elusive triadic synapse, to building spiking neural network models of the ventral visual pathway to investigate a novel mechanism for solving the feature binding problem. After graduating, I interned with Emilie Macé, where I used functional ultrasound imaging (fUSI) to record whole-brain activity in awake behaving mice and analysed it to find correlates of behavioural variables. I then joined CoMind Technologies worked as a Research Engineer at CoMind Technologies, helping to develop non-invasive optical neural interfaces. I worked in the data science team on a range of problems, from developing deep learning models for artefact detection to analysing functional imaging experiments, before leaving to pursue a PhD in neuroscience.
